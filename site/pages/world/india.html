Money basics in India

The Indian rules and accounts most families meet, in plain words. Most MoneyViel examples already use India, so you are in the right place.

📌 Local things worth knowing

🧾 Income tax and your PANSalaried people usually have tax deducted at source (TDS). You file your return yourself on the official portal, and choose between the old and new tax regimes.Check current rules: incometax.gov.in
🏦 EPF for salaried workersIf your employer is covered, part of your salary goes into the Employees' Provident Fund each month, with a matching employer share. You can track it with your UAN.Check current rules: epfindia.gov.in
🏛️ PPF and NPSThe Public Provident Fund is a long-term, government-backed savings scheme. The National Pension System is a retirement account invested in the markets.Check current rules: npstrust.org.in
🛡️ Bank deposits are insured up to a limitDeposit insurance (DICGC) protects each depositor at each bank up to a set amount, so very large savings may be safer split across banks.Check current rules: dicgc.org.in
📊 Your credit scoreLenders check your credit report before giving loans and cards. You are entitled to a free copy from each credit bureau once a year.Check current rules: rbi.org.in
🚨 Check before you investUse SEBI to check whether an adviser or broker is registered, and RBI's Sachet portal to report deposit schemes that look suspicious.Check current rules: sachet.rbi.org.in

✅ Your next three steps

  1. Build an emergency fund of a few months of essential costs in a savings account or sweep FD.
  2. List every loan and card with its interest rate, and clear the most expensive one first.
  3. Check your EPF passbook and credit report once a year so nothing surprises you.

🌍 What works the same everywhere

🛟 A safety cushion firstKeep a few months of essential costs in a safe, easy-to-reach account before you take any risks.
💳 Minimum payments keep you in debtPaying only the minimum on a credit card can stretch a small bill over many years. Our race shows how.
📈 Time beats timingSmall amounts invested regularly for many years usually matter more than picking the perfect moment.
🚩 If it sounds too good, walk awayGuaranteed high returns, pressure to decide today and "keep this secret" are classic signs of a scam.
