## Email 2 (1 day later)
Subject: Day 2: Needs, wants and the 50/30/20 starting point
Preview: The simplest budget that actually sticks.

Hi {{FirstName | there}},

Yesterday you saw where your money went. Today, sort it.

Every spend is a **need** (rent, food, bills, school fees, loan payments) or a **want** (eating out, shopping, upgrades). Both are fine. Problems start when wants quietly eat the money meant for the future.

**A simple starting point: 50/30/20**
- About 50% of take-home pay for needs
- About 30% for wants
- At least 20% for saving and paying off debt

It's a guide, not a rule. If rent is high where you live, needs may be 60% for now. The real goal: **pay your future first** by moving your saving amount on salary day, before you spend.

**Today's step:** mark each of yesterday's 5 big spends as Need or Want. Then decide one saving amount and set an automatic transfer for salary day.

## Email 3 (1 day later)
Subject: Day 3: The "minimum due" trap (it costs more than you think)
Preview: 14 years for one credit card bill?

Hi {{FirstName | there}},

Credit card statements show a small "minimum amount due". Paying it keeps you out of late fees, but it keeps the debt alive.

**An example we checked:** a ₹80,000 card balance at 36% a year interest, paying only a 5% minimum (at least ₹200) each month and spending nothing new, takes about **14 years** to clear and costs over **₹1,00,000 in interest**, before GST. The maths is the same in any currency.

## Email 4 (1 day later)
Subject: Day 4: Make a plan to clear every debt
Preview: Avalanche or Snowball: which one fits you?

Hi {{FirstName | there}},

If you have more than one loan or card, the order you pay them matters.

- **Avalanche:** pay the minimum on everything, and put every extra rupee on the debt with the **highest interest rate**. Usually saves the most interest.
- **Snowball:** put the extra on the **smallest balance** first. You clear one debt quickly, and that win keeps you going.

Both work. The best plan is the one you will stick with.

**Today's step:** list every debt (balance, interest rate, minimum payment), then let the planner build your month-by-month plan and debt-free date.
⛓️ Debt Payoff Planner: https://moneyviel.com/money-tools/debt-payoff-planner/

Struggling to pay? Talk to your lender early. Many can offer a payment plan.

## Email 5 (1 day later)
Subject: Day 5: Your safety net (so a bad month doesn't become debt)
Preview: How much emergency money do you really need?

Hi {{FirstName | there}},

Most debt starts with a surprise: a hospital bill, a job loss, a repair. An emergency fund turns a crisis into an inconvenience.

**A common guide:** keep **3 to 6 months of essential costs** (needs only, not wants) in a safe place you can reach quickly, such as a separate savings account. More if your income is irregular or one person earns for the family.

Start small. Even one month's needs is a real win.

**Today's step:** work out your target and how long it will take at a monthly amount you can manage.
