**Chapter 2: Needs and wants**

The next evening, Victor and Mira sort every spend into two columns:
- **Needs:** rent, groceries, Nia's fees, bills, transport, loan payments
- **Wants:** food delivery, eating out, shopping, three streaming subscriptions they barely watch

Wants aren't bad. The problem is when wants quietly eat the money meant for the future.

They make two decisions:
1. Halve food delivery and eating out: ₹3,900 a month saved.
2. Cancel the unused subscriptions: ₹1,200 a month.

That's ₹5,100. Add the ₹1,900 that was left over anyway, and they have **₹7,000 a month** to work with. That's about a twelfth of their pay, found without earning more.

Next, Mira sets an **automatic transfer of ₹3,000 on payday** into a separate savings account. Money for the future moves *first*, before anything else can spend it. The other ₹4,000 has an important job, which you'll see tomorrow.

**A simple guide many people start with: 50/30/20.** About 50% of take-home pay for needs, about 30% for wants, and at least 20% for saving and paying off debt. It's a starting point, not a strict rule. If rent is high where you live, start where you can and build up.

## Chapter 4 (1 day later)
Subject: Chapter 4: Two debts, one choice
Preview: Avalanche or Snowball: which one would you pick?

Hi {{FirstName | there}},

**Chapter 4: Which debt first?**

The card isn't their only debt. Victor's bike loan still has ₹45,000 left at 11% a year, paid at ₹3,000 a month. So which debt should get their extra money?

There are two popular methods:
- 🏔️ **Avalanche:** keep paying the minimum or regular payment on every debt, and put every extra bit of money on the debt with the **highest interest rate**. This usually saves the most interest.
- ⛄ **Snowball:** pay the minimum or regular payment on everything, and put the extra on the **smallest balance** first. You clear one debt quickly, and that win keeps you motivated.

For Victor and Mira, Snowball would mean the bike loan first (it's the smaller balance). Avalanche means the credit card first (36% a year vs 11%, more than three times the rate). The gap is huge, so they choose **Avalanche**: the extra goes to the card, and the bike payment continues as normal.

Both methods work. The best plan is the one you will actually stick with.

**A common guide:** keep **3 to 6 months of must-pay costs** (needs only, not wants) somewhere safe and easy to reach, such as a separate savings account. Keep more if your income is irregular or one person earns for the whole family.
