Chapter 3: The minimum-due trap

Setting up their first home cost more than they planned, and Victor, who finds it hard to say no, put a lot of it on the credit card. Now they owe ₹80,000 on a card that charges 3% a month (36% a year). Every month they pay the "minimum due", about ₹4,000. It feels responsible. No late fees, no calls.

Victor puts their numbers into our Debt-Free Grand Prix and watches the cars race:

  • 🔴 Minimum due only: about 14 years to clear the card, and about ₹108,000 in interest. That's more than the card balance itself.
  • 🟡 A fixed ₹8,000 a month (the ₹4,000 minimum plus the extra ₹4,000 from Chapter 2): clear in 13 months, with about ₹16,500 in interest.

Same card, same couple. Paying a fixed amount instead of the shrinking minimum saves them more than ₹90,000 in interest. They stop using the card for new spending and set up a fixed ₹8,000 auto-payment.

(These figures are examples. They assume no new spending on the card and a minimum due of 5% of the balance, and they don't include taxes that some countries add to card interest, such as India's 18% GST. With those taxes, both costs are higher.)

No card debt? Set your card to auto-pay the full statement amount every month, and keep enough in your bank account to cover it. Paying the full statement by the due date means no interest on your purchases. (Cash withdrawals on a card are usually charged interest from day one, so avoid them.)
