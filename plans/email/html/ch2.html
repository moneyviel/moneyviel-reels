Chapter 2: Needs and wants

The next evening, Riya and Arjun sort every spend into two columns:

  • Needs: rent, groceries, school fees, bills, transport, loan payments
  • Wants: food delivery, eating out, shopping, three streaming subscriptions they barely watch

Wants aren't bad. The problem is when wants quietly eat the money meant for the future.

They make two decisions:

  1. Halve food delivery and eating out: ₹3,900 a month saved.
  2. Cancel the unused subscriptions: ₹1,200 a month.

That's ₹5,100. Add the ₹1,900 that was left over anyway, and they have ₹7,000 a month to work with.

Next, Arjun sets an automatic transfer of ₹3,000 on salary day into a separate savings account. Money for the future moves *first*, before anything else can spend it. The other ₹4,000 has an important job, which you'll see tomorrow.

A simple guide many people start with: 50/30/20. About 50% of take-home pay for needs, about 30% for wants, and at least 20% for saving and paying off debt. It's a starting point, not a strict rule. If rent is high where you live, start where you can and build up.
