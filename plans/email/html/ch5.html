Chapter 5: The safety net

On a Sunday morning, Grandma Rosa slips on the stairs. After the doctor's visit, an X-ray and medicines, the bill is ₹9,000, and Victor and Mira want to cover it. Thankfully, she's back to her sharp-eyed self within a few days.

Before this week, they would have put it on the credit card and undone their plan. This time Mira remembers ₹15,000 sitting in an old savings account. They pay the bill from there, and the card plan stays on track.

It makes them ask: how much should a safety net be?

A common guide: keep 3 to 6 months of must-pay costs (needs only, not wants) somewhere safe and easy to reach, such as a separate savings account. Keep more if your income is irregular or one person earns for the whole family.

Their must-pay costs (rent, groceries, Nia's fees, bills, transport and the bike loan) add up to ₹51,000 a month. (They leave out the credit card because it will be gone in about a year. Until then, a fund that also covers its minimum payment would be safer.) So their target is ₹153,000 (3 months) to ₹306,000 (6 months).

That sounds huge, so they break it down. The ₹6,000 left in the old account becomes the start of their safety net. First stop: one month, ₹51,000. With ₹45,000 still to go, ₹3,000 a month alone would take 15 months. But the card will be cleared in about 13 months, and then its ₹8,000 payment can join, making it ₹11,000 a month. So they reach ₹51,000 in about 14 months.
